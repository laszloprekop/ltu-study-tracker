## Corrections to existing cards

All eight are in `data/cards/2026-10-03-ip-and-patterns.json`. None is wrong in substance; these are precision, wording and source fixes.

1. **What is the difference between forwarding and routing in the network layer?** "Routers exchange information and fill those tables" describes only the traditional per-router control plane. The L08 slides give two approaches: routing algorithms in the routers, or an SDN remote controller that computes and installs the tables. Sources are `4.2` and `1.4`; `4.1` fits better as the second source, since the card answers a learning objective and the first Study Guide focus question.
2. **What is the TTL field in the IPv4 header for?** "At 0, the router throws the packet away and tells the sender" goes beyond Module 4: the L08 slide says only "remaining max hops (decremented at each router)", and the Module 4 material says of ICMP only that it does error reporting. The notice to the sender belongs with traceroute (Module 1, not checked for this audit). Drop "and tells the sender", or add the Module 1 item that teaches it as a source.
3. **A router has two routes that match 10.1.2.3: 10.0.0.0/8 and 10.1.0.0/16 ...** Correct. The prompt is 124 characters, over the 120 limit used for new cards. The slides work the rule in binary with a four-row table and an "otherwise" row; the card's dotted example is fine but is not the slide's.
4. **What does the /n in an IPv4 address such as 192.168.1.0/24 say?** Correct. The slides call the two parts "subnet part" and "host part" and name the notation CIDR (`a.b.c.d/x`); the card says "network part". Using the slide terms once would help students find it again. Key line "a bigger /n means a smaller network" is right.
5. **How many usable host addresses does a /26 subnet have, and why not 64?** Correct. In Module 4 the minus 2 is only said aloud (the Lecture 8 transcript: two addresses of every subnet have fixed purposes, one of them broadcast; "2 to the power of 8 minus 2"), it is not on the L08 slides. The source `9.7` carries it; no change needed.
6. **What does a NAT router change in an outgoing datagram, and what does it remember?** Correct. The example host is `10.0.0.5:3345`; the L09 slide uses `10.0.0.1, 3345` to `138.76.29.7, 5001`, so the card mixes the slide's ports and public address with another host. Use `10.0.0.1`. "Many home devices" is narrower than the slides (home, institutional and 4G/5G cellular networks). Add `4.1`.
7. **Name two ways IPv6 simplified the header compared with IPv4.** The prompt asks for two, the answer lists four, and the fourth (addresses grow from 32 to 128 bits) is not a simplification. Three reasons are not the slides': "no header checksum: the link and transport layers already check" (slide: "to speed processing at routers"), "the sender sizes them right" (slide: "no fragmentation/reassembly"; the author's video: done at the end points), and "extension headers" (slide: options "available as upper-layer, next-header protocol"). Suggested prompt: "Which three things did IPv6 remove from the IPv4 header, and why?" Add `4.1`.
8. **What is tunnelling in the move from IPv4 to IPv6?** Correct. The slides spell it "tunneling". It could say who builds the tunnel: the routers at the two ends speak both IPv4 and IPv6 (B and E on the slide); the new tunnel-address card covers it.

The 4.3 cards rest on the slides, with the captions used to read the slide figures (the tunnel example, the flow table examples, the load balancer as a Layer 7 switch).

The cards follow the slides.
- **Priority classes.** The slides say any header field can be used to classify traffic; the recording adds that each ISP decides what counts as high priority. The cards state only the scheduling rules.

- **Subnet arithmetic.** The L08 slides teach what a subnet and a prefix are, not how to compute ranges, masks or host counts. Those are in the existing `9.7` cards; no new card repeats them. The Study Guide asks students to "determine network ranges", and the Subnet Addressing problem practises it.
